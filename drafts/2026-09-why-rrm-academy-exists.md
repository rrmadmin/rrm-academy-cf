I spent four and a half years in OBGYN training before I could diagnose my own endometriosis. I had the textbooks. I had the rotations. I had the pain every month. Nobody put the three together, including me.

That is the gap RRM Academy was built to close.

## What is RRM Academy?

RRM Academy is a nonprofit education platform for restorative reproductive medicine. We publish patient guides, a research library, a glossary, courses for clinicians, and a directory of clinicians who practice restoratively. The guides, the library, and the glossary are free to read. The organization behind the site is the Restorative Reproductive Medicine Foundation, a 501(c)(3) public charity.

Restorative reproductive medicine is a field, not a single protocol. The clinicians in it share a set of principles. Find the underlying cause. Cooperate with a woman's physiology instead of overriding it. Treat the cycle as real clinical data. Evaluate both partners. Do the evaluation before choosing the treatment. The specific methods vary from one training tradition to the next. The principles hold across all of them.

## Why does RRM Academy exist?

Women get told their pain is normal. They get told their cycles are just difficult. They get told nothing is wrong when something clearly is. This is where patients are gaslit, especially women.

The years that pass after that are not waiting on better technology. The imaging exists. The lab work exists. The surgical skill exists. What is missing is somebody willing to look, and a patient who knows enough to keep asking until someone does.

So how do you overcome gaslighting? Education. Awareness, so a woman can build the confidence to stand up to it, ask a sharper question, and go get a second opinion. Education is the whole reason this site is here.

## Why does RRM Academy highlight the people fixing women's health?

There is a lot of predatory material in fertility and women's health. Products sold on fear. Programs sold on urgency. Promises made to people at the most desperate moment of their lives, by a market that does very well when women stay confused. From the outside, the honest work and the marketing look almost identical.

So we name the people doing the honest work. The clinicians, surgeons, researchers, and advocates we feature here are the change in women's health we want to see, bridging care and awareness and research gaps. Making it clear who those people are, from knowledge and from science, is part of the job.

## Is a diagnosis something to be afraid of?

Most women brace for the name. The name is the good part.

A diagnosis gives you something to work with. Name it to tame it and face it and find a treatment or resolution. Endometriosis has a workup and a surgical answer in excision. PCOS, now also called PMOS, for Polyendocrine Metabolic Ovarian Syndrome, has a metabolic and endocrine workup. Adenomyosis, thyroid disease, a luteal phase problem, a sperm problem: each one has a path forward once it has a name.

"Unexplained" has no path. Unexplained infertility is undiagnosed, not unexplained, and the word does more damage than the diagnosis it is standing in for. Hope starts on the day somebody finally tells you what is actually happening.

## What is wrong with quick fixes in women's health?

A quick fix is anything that skips the diagnosis, and this field is full of them on every side.

Suppressive medications handed to a fifteen-year-old with severe pain, with nobody looking for what is causing the pain. IVF offered to a couple whose reason for not conceiving was never established, going around the problem and leaving it in the body. A supplement stack bought online for symptoms no one has evaluated. A GLP-1 started for a metabolic picture no one has worked up.

Different price tags. The same move. Something gets prescribed, and the cause stays where it was.

Healing runs on an algorithm, and it is the same one used everywhere else in medicine. Find the cause. Name it. Treat it. Then measure whether the person in front of you actually got better.

## Why does RRM Academy say ovulation is healthy?

My first real introduction to women's health in medical school was a lecture explaining that ovulation raises your risk of cancer. Sit with that. The opening lesson was that a woman's own healthy state is a problem in itself, and that the sensible thing to do about a working reproductive system is to switch it off.

Ovulation is healthy. It is how a woman makes her own progesterone, and progesterone does work throughout the body, well beyond the uterus. A cycle that ovulates well tells you that a great many other systems are functioning. The ovulatory cycle belongs on the list of vital signs for exactly that reason, and a clinician working without cycle data is flying blind.

We teach the cycle as data because it is data, and because most clinicians were never shown how to read it.

## Who is RRM Academy for?

Women who have been handed a prescription and no explanation. Couples working through infertility, both partners, because fertility belongs to two people and the evaluation should too. Mothers and daughters who want to know what a normal cycle actually looks like before something goes wrong. Women in their forties and fifties being told that everything they are feeling is simply their age.

And clinicians. Medical students, residents, family doctors, OBGYNs, midwives, nurses, all of whom received close to no training in fertility awareness or in reading a chart, and many of whom want that training now.

You do not need any charting background to start here. Most people arrive with none.

## What RRM Academy is not

We are not a clinic. Nothing on this site is medical advice, and reading a guide does not make me your doctor. I am not seeing patients through RRM Academy. The way to find care is the clinician directory at [rrmacademy.org/providers](https://rrmacademy.org/providers/).

We do not sell supplements, devices, or at-home test kits, and we do not publish dosing schedules or protocol specifics. Those belong in a consultation with a clinician who has examined you and knows your history. What belongs here is the education that lets you walk into that consultation prepared.

We are also not neutral. This site takes a clear position: reproductive disease should be diagnosed and treated, and a woman's physiology deserves to be understood before anyone decides to suppress it or work around it.

## Who is behind RRM Academy?

I am a board-certified OBGYN and a fellowship-trained restorative surgeon. I trained in the conventional model first, all four years of it, and then trained in the restorative one. I have operated on endometriosis, counseled couples through infertility, and been the patient sitting on the table wondering why nobody had connected the obvious.

I am not the whole organization. RRM Academy is a program of the Restorative Reproductive Medicine Foundation, and the work here is shared with other clinicians, researchers, editors, and advocates who care about the same gaps. Our content carries a byline and a review line so you always know who is accountable for what you are reading.

## Where to start

Restorative points forward. The default story tells a woman that her body failed and technology has to take over from here. The restorative story is about restoring your health for the seasons ahead: your fertility, and also the decades that follow it, when your hormones are still doing quiet work in your bones, your brain, and your heart.

If you are new to this, begin with [What is Restorative Reproductive Medicine](https://rrmacademy.org/what-is-rrm/). If you already know what you are looking at and want a clinician, go to the [provider directory](https://rrmacademy.org/providers/). If you are a clinician who suspects your training left something out, it did, and the courses are here for that.

Women deserve better. Women deserve answers.
