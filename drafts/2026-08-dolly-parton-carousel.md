**ATTRIBUTION NOTE:** her infertility is her own stated fact (1982, 1987). Endometriosis is the
reported diagnosis: multiple mainstream outlets, and the Endometriosis Foundation of America, list
her among the public figures who faced endometriosis and infertility. She never used the word in a
verifiable first-person quote. Slide 5 carries that clause and it must not be cut in design review.
The treatment critique on slide 6 is aimed at the era's paradigm, never at her doctors or her choices.

## Slide 1 (hook)

**Dolly Parton talked about her infertility when almost nobody did.**

1982. Her own words.

---

## Slide 2

**"I can't have children, but I wasn't able to before, either."**

Dolly Parton, UPI interview, 1982

She was 35 when she got seriously ill. She collapsed on tour.

---

## Slide 3

**"If I could have had children, I probably wouldn't have had the female problems I've had."**

It was the only language anyone had given her for it.

---

## Slide 4

**Mid-1980s: a partial hysterectomy.**

"Suddenly I was a middle-aged woman."

"I went through a dark time, until I made myself snap out of it."

---

## Slide 5

**The evidence points to endometriosis.**

Mainstream outlets and the Endometriosis Foundation of America list her among the women who faced it, though she never used the word herself.

A generation got a euphemism where a diagnosis belonged.

---

## Slide 6

**Endometriosis lives OUTSIDE the uterus.**

On the peritoneum. The ovaries. The bowel.

Taking the uterus does not take the disease.

Take the organ was the era's answer to a woman in pain.

---

## Slide 7

**Women are still waiting.**

Median delay from first symptoms to an endometriosis diagnosis: 9 years.
(Pugsley and Ballard, 2007)

Restorative care aims to find the disease, remove it completely, and keep the organs.

---

## Slide 8 (close)

**She talked about it for forty years.**

That took nerve in 1982.

## Caption (56 words)

The slides carry the story. The caption's only job is the one thought the slides never say:
fame broke the silence and still could not move the medicine.

Dolly Parton died on August 25 at 80.

Money. Access. A national platform.

In 1982 all of it bought her the same medicine every other woman in pain got.

Fame moves a lot of things. It does not move what medicine believes.

Changing that belief is the work.

Thank you, Dolly. 💜
