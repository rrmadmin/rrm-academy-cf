Reading List

The Best Books on Restorative Reproductive Medicine, Fertility Awareness & Hormone Health

A curated reading list for anyone learning to work with the body instead of around it: the books we would hand a new patient, a charting student, or a clinician exploring restorative care.

NWCurated by RRM Academy · Reviewed by Dr. Naomi Whittaker, MD ✓ No affiliate links Updated June 2026

Restorative reproductive medicine (RRM) starts from a simple idea: the menstrual cycle is a vital sign, and most reproductive problems have an underlying cause worth finding and treating, rather than overriding. The books below teach that worldview from every angle, from the clinical foundations of NaProTechnology, to learning to read your own cycle, to the nutrition and hormone science that supports it. We have organized them by what you actually want to do, and we flag, plainly, where a popular book's framing differs from the restorative approach. We earn nothing from these recommendations.

The Foundations

Understand RRM & NaProTechnology

The source texts. Start here to understand what restorative reproductive medicine is and where it came from.

01

The NaProTechnology Revolution: Unleashing the Power in a Woman's Cycle

Thomas W. Hilgers, MD · Beaufort Books
2010For everyone

The single best lay introduction to NaProTechnology, the medical and surgical core of restorative reproductive medicine, written by the physician who developed it. Hilgers shows how charting the cycle reveals the real causes of infertility, miscarriage, PCOS, endometriosis and PMS, and how to treat those causes instead of bypassing them.

Good to know: Written before the term "RRM" came into use, and from an explicitly faith-based (Pope Paul VI Institute) tradition. Treat its head-to-head success-rate claims as the author's; the underlying outcomes are better cited from the peer-reviewed studies.
Find it on Bookshop
02

The Medical & Surgical Practice of NaProTechnology

Thomas W. Hilgers, MD · Pope Paul VI Institute Press
2004For cliniciansTextbook · 1,244 pp

The definitive clinical reference for NaProTechnology: hormonal evaluation of the cycle, ovulatory and luteal-phase disorders, infertility, miscarriage, and the gynecologic surgery behind restorative care, all built on Creighton Model charting as the diagnostic framework. This is the physician's textbook.

Find it on Bookshop
03

NeoFertility: Advanced Fertility Care Through Cycle Tracking

Dr. Phil Boyle, MD · User Manual
2020For patientsShort manual

A concise patient companion from Dr. Phil Boyle, a leading RRM physician and president of the International Institute for Restorative Reproductive Medicine. It teaches the cycle-charting that underpins his NeoFertility approach to infertility, recurrent miscarriage and gynecologic health.

Find it
Body Literacy

Learn to Read Your Cycle

Fertility-awareness charting is the skill at the center of RRM. These teach you to observe and interpret your own biomarkers.

04

The Fifth Vital Sign: Master Your Cycles & Optimize Your Fertility

Lisa Hendrickson-Jack · Fertility Friday Publishing
2019For everyone

The book that makes the full case for treating the menstrual cycle as a vital sign of overall health. Heavily referenced (over 1,000 citations), it teaches cycle literacy and argues that suppressing the cycle hides information your body is working to give you, the conceptual heart of the restorative view.

Good to know: A secular fertility-awareness book that also covers using charting to avoid pregnancy. Read it for the cycle-as-vital-sign and anti-suppression argument; it complements, rather than replaces, instruction in a specific method.

05

The Art of Natural Family Planning

John & Sheila Kippley · Couple to Couple League
1996 (4th ed.)Charting students

The classic manual of the sympto-thermal method: cross-checking basal body temperature against cervical-mucus observations to map the fertile and infertile phases. For decades this has been the text many couples first learn to chart from.

Find it on Bookshop
06

Living with Your Fertility: The Marquette Method of NFP

Richard Fehring, Mary Schneider & Mary Lee Barron · Marquette University
2023Teachers & learners

The teaching text for the Marquette Method, which pairs cycle observation with a home urinary-hormone monitor for an added objective marker. Written for the professionals who teach it, but accessible to committed self-learners, and one of the more recent, evidence-forward FABM texts.

Find it on Bookshop
07

The Billings Method: Controlling Fertility Without Drugs or Devices

Dr. Evelyn Billings & Ann Westmore
1980 (updated eds.)For everyone

From the physicians who pioneered cervical-mucus charting, the original guide to the Billings Ovulation Method, a cornerstone of modern fertility awareness that has been translated into more than 20 languages.

Good to know: The older "controlling fertility" subtitle frames the method as natural birth control. RRM uses the same mucus observations as a diagnostic window into reproductive health.
Find it on Bookshop
08

Taking Charge of Your Fertility

Toni Weschler, MPH · William Morrow
1995 · 20th-anniv. 2015For everyone

The best-known fertility-awareness book in print and a superb on-ramp to body literacy, teaching observation of the three primary fertility signs: waking temperature, cervical fluid and cervical position.

How it differs from RRM: A secular, self-taught symptothermal approach framed partly as "natural birth control." RRM relies on a standardized, instructor-taught method whose chart feeds a clinician's diagnosis and treatment of the underlying cause, not a self-managed timing tool.
Find it on Bookshop
09

The Garden of Fertility

Katie Singer · Avery
2004For patients

A warm, approachable guide to charting your fertility signs and reading them as a window into reproductive and gynecologic health, a gentle first book for many people new to cycle awareness.

How it differs from RRM: A secular fertility-awareness primer framed for both avoiding and achieving pregnancy. Read it as body-literacy education, not clinical guidance.
Find it on Bookshop
10

Cycle Savvy: The Smart Teen's Guide to the Mysteries of Her Body

Toni Weschler, MPH · Harper
2006For teens

A cycle-literacy guide written for teenage girls, a gentle, age-appropriate first introduction to menstruation, ovulation and the body's natural rhythms. A thoughtful gift for a daughter learning what a healthy cycle looks like.

Find it on Bookshop
Root-Cause Health

Heal Your Hormones

Popular, root-cause books on periods and hormones. They share RRM's restore-don't-suppress instinct, with the caveats noted.

11

Period Repair Manual: Natural Treatment for Better Hormones and Better Periods

Lara Briden, ND · GreenPeak Publishing
2017 (2nd ed.)For patients

The most popular root-cause guide to period and hormone problems, PCOS, endometriosis, PMS and missing periods, with an approach built on restoring real ovulatory cycles rather than masking symptoms. A natural companion to RRM's philosophy.

Good to know: Naturopathic and supplement-forward; the author is an ND. Specific dosing suggestions are not RRM protocols, but the underlying restore-the-cycle posture aligns well.
Find it on Bookshop
12

Beyond the Pill

Dr. Jolene Brighten, NMD · HarperOne
2019For patients

A practical 30-day program for women coming off hormonal birth control, focused on restoring natural hormone function through diet, targeted nutrients and lifestyle.

Good to know: Naturopathic; the "post-birth-control syndrome" construct and some protocols are not established in conventional or RRM literature. Useful for the come-off-the-pill transition, not as clinical authority.
Find it on Bookshop
13

Fix Your Period

Nicole Jardim · Harper Wave
2020For patients

A six-week, symptom-by-symptom program for cramps, bloating, PMS, irregular cycles and mood, framed around the cycle as a barometer of health and explicitly positioned "beyond birth control."

Good to know: Written by a functional-health "period coach," not a physician. Treat its self-directed supplement and diet protocols as popular wellness guidance.
Find it on Bookshop
14

Is This Normal? Judgment-Free Straight Talk About Your Body

Dr. Jolene Brighten, NMD · Simon Element
2023For patients

Plain, frank answers to the body questions women actually have, about cycles, hormones, fertility and gynecologic symptoms, with a cycle-aware lens and a 28-day plan. A friendly entry point to "is my cycle telling me something?"

Find it on Bookshop
15

WomanCode

Alisa Vitti · HarperOne
2013For patients

A widely read introduction to eating and living in tune with your cycle to address PMS, irregular cycles, fertility and energy, the book that popularized "cycle syncing."

Good to know: Largely anecdotal and broad in its claims, and it uses older "PCOS" language (RRM Academy is moving toward "PMOS"). Useful for cycle awareness, not as medical evidence.
Find it on Bookshop
Condition-Specific

PCOS & Endometriosis

The two conditions RRM patients most often research. These respect the root-cause and, for endometriosis, the excision-first approach.

16

8 Steps to Reverse Your PCOS

Dr. Fiona McCulloch, ND · Greenleaf
2016For patients

A root-cause program for PCOS that targets the real drivers, insulin resistance, inflammation, excess androgens and thyroid, to restore ovulation and fertility rather than mask symptoms.

Good to know: Naturopathic and supplement-forward. RRM Academy increasingly uses "PMOS" (polycystic metabolic-ovarian syndrome) for this condition; the book predates that shift.
Find it on Bookshop
17

Beating Endo: How to Reclaim Your Life from Endometriosis

Iris Kerin Orbuch, MD & Amy Stein, DPT · Harper Wave
2019For patients

Co-written by an excision surgeon and a pelvic-floor physical therapist, the clearest patient guide to treating endometriosis as a whole-body disease, with complete excision surgery (explicitly over ablation) and pelvic-floor care at the center.

Good to know: It presents hormonal suppression (continuous pills, GnRH agonists) as standard symptom management. RRM views suppression as masking, not treating, the disease, but the book's excision-first, multidisciplinary core is strongly aligned.
Find it on Bookshop
18

Heal Endo: An Anti-Inflammatory Approach to Healing from Endometriosis

Katie Edmonds, NTP
2022For patients

An anti-inflammatory, whole-body approach to living with endometriosis, covering diet, gut health, environment and surgical choices, with real respect for the role and the quality of excision surgery.

Good to know: Written by a nutritional therapist, not a physician. Strong on lifestyle and on why the type of surgery matters; verify any specific clinical claim.
Find it on Bookshop
19

The Endometriosis Health and Diet Program

Andrew S. Cook, MD & Danielle Cook, MS, RD · Robert Rose
2017For patients

A surgeon-and-dietitian guide that pairs a clear medical explanation of endometriosis with an anti-inflammatory eating plan and roughly 100 recipes, useful complementary reading alongside surgical care.

Find it on Bookshop
Preconception & Pregnancy

Nutrition for Fertility

Food and egg-quality science. These support conception by improving the underlying terrain, fully in keeping with restorative care.

20

Real Food for Fertility

Lily Nichols, RDN & Lisa Hendrickson-Jack · Fertility Food Publishing
2024For patients

A recent, heavily referenced preconception guide that pairs evidence-based nutrition for egg and sperm quality with fertility-awareness charting, two RRM pillars in a single book, co-authored by a research dietitian and a fertility-awareness educator.

Find it on Bookshop
21

Real Food for Pregnancy

Lily Nichols, RDN, CDE
2018For patients

A meticulously referenced (900+ citations) guide to optimal nutrition through pregnancy, widely adopted by midwives and clinicians, and a practical complement to restorative preconception care.

Find it on Bookshop
22

It Starts with the Egg

Rebecca Fett · Franklin Fox
2014 · rev. eds.For patients

The most accessible synthesis of the egg-quality research: which supplements, foods and environmental exposures actually move the needle for conception and miscarriage prevention.

Good to know: The subtitle and parts of the book frame egg quality around improving IVF odds. Read it for the natural-conception and miscarriage-prevention chapters, which stand on their own.
Find it on Bookshop
23

The Fertility Diet

Jorge Chavarro, MD & Walter Willett, MD · McGraw-Hill
2007For patients

Harvard researchers translate findings from the Nurses' Health Study into practical diet and lifestyle changes that support ovulation, especially helpful for ovulatory and PCOS-pattern subfertility.

Good to know: The evidence is observational, so read it as associational lifestyle guidance rather than clinical protocol.
Find it on Bookshop
Perimenopause & Beyond

Hormones at Midlife

The cycle is a vital sign right up to its close. Two reads for the perimenopause transition.

24

Hormone Repair Manual: Every Woman's Guide to Healthy Hormones After 40

Lara Briden, ND · GreenPeak Publishing
2021For patients

Briden's guide to the perimenopause transition, treating the cycle as a vital sign through its natural close and emphasizing root-cause, lifestyle and body-identical hormone support. The companion to her Period Repair Manual.

Find it on Bookshop
25

The New Menopause

Mary Claire Haver, MD · Rodale
2024For patients

A #1 bestseller and a practical, A-to-Z reference for perimenopause and menopause symptoms and options, from a board-certified OB/GYN.

Good to know: Centers menopausal hormone therapy as the default intervention, a conventional rather than a restorative, root-cause frame. A useful reference, read with that lens.
Find it on Bookshop
Wider Reading

Also Worth Knowing

Adjacent titles that come up often, useful for context even where they sit outside the restorative model.

Honoring Our Cycles

— Katie Singer (2006)

A short, plain-language fertility-awareness charting workbook; a lighter companion to The Garden of Fertility.

Sweetening the Pill

— Holly Grigg-Spall (2013)

The cultural critique of hormonal birth control that sparked the documentary The Business of Birth Control. Advocacy, not clinical guidance, but influential.

In the FLO

— Alisa Vitti (2020)

Cycle-syncing diet, exercise and work to the phases of the cycle. Popular and cycle-affirming; some claims run ahead of the evidence.

Estrogen Matters

— Avrum Bluming, MD & Carol Tavris, PhD (2024 rev.)

A reexamination of the hormone-therapy safety debate. About menopausal HRT rather than the reproductive cycle, but frequently cited.

The Menopause Brain

— Lisa Mosconi, PhD (2024)

The neuroscience of how the menopause transition reshapes the brain, from a respected researcher.

How we chose these, and how we make money on them

This list is curated by RRM Academy and reviewed by Dr. Naomi Whittaker. We included books that teach restorative reproductive medicine and the body literacy around it well, and we excluded books that frame hormonal suppression as a cure. Where a popular book carries framing that differs from the restorative approach, we say so plainly rather than leave it out, because an honest reading list is more useful than a tidy one.

We use no affiliate links and accept no payment from publishers or authors. The "Find it" links point to Bookshop.org, which supports independent bookstores, and we earn nothing when you buy. These are simply the books we would hand a patient or a new student.
