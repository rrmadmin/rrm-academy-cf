### /commentary/uterine-isthmocele-c-section-scar-restorative-solutions/

```markdown
## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is an isthmocele?
An isthmocele, also called a cesarean scar defect, is a pouch-like recess that forms at the site of a uterine incision from a prior cesarean section. The myometrium does not heal completely, leaving a niche in the lower uterine segment. This structural defect can collect fluid, menstrual blood, and inflammatory debris.

### What are the symptoms of an isthmocele?
Symptoms include abnormal uterine bleeding (especially postmenstrual spotting), chronic pelvic pain, dysmenorrhea, and dyspareunia. Some women also experience secondary infertility or recurrent early pregnancy loss. Symptom severity does not always correspond to defect size; a small defect can produce significant symptoms, and a larger one can be incidentally discovered.

### How is an isthmocele diagnosed?
Transvaginal ultrasound is typically the first imaging step. Saline infusion sonohysterography and hysteroscopy allow direct visualization of the defect and more precise measurement of residual myometrial thickness. MRI provides additional anatomical detail when surgical planning requires it.

### Can an isthmocele cause infertility?
In many cases, the defect plays a real role. Fluid and inflammatory debris trapped in the niche can impair sperm transport, disrupt implantation, and contribute to recurrent early loss. Published series document improved reproductive outcomes following repair (Harjee et al., 2021). The defect is structural. It can be corrected.

### How is an isthmocele treated?
Surgical repair is the standard approach. Both hysteroscopic and laparoscopic techniques are used, with the choice depending on residual myometrial thickness and the extent of the defect. The goal is to restore normal uterine architecture, eliminate the fluid-collecting niche, and re-establish an intact lower uterine segment. An RRM clinician evaluates structural, hormonal, and cycle-level factors together, rather than addressing the scar in isolation. Find an RRM clinician at /providers/.
```

### /commentary/naprotechnology-surgery-a-restorative-approach-to-fertility-and-gynecologic-health/

```markdown
## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is NaProTechnology evidence-based?
NaProTechnology has a published evidence base including peer-reviewed cohort studies on infertility, recurrent pregnancy loss, endometriosis, and hormonal disorders. Sánchez-Méndez and colleagues (2025) followed 1,310 couples and reported a 62.1% live birth rate at 36 or more months. The evidence is largely observational. So is most of IVF's foundational literature.

### What are the criticisms of NaProTechnology, and how does RRM respond?
The most common criticism is the absence of randomized controlled trials. That standard applies with equal force to IVF: a 2023 Cochrane review found only 2 RCTs enrolling 86 women comparing IVF to expectant management. A second criticism is that the approach is faith-based. NaProTechnology was developed by Dr. Thomas W. Hilgers and is practiced across secular clinical settings; its validity rests on outcomes data.

### Does NaProTechnology surgery work for infertility?
In many cases, surgery within a NaProTechnology-informed framework addresses the condition actually causing the fertility problem. Excision of endometriosis, repair of tubal obstruction, and correction of structural abnormalities restore the conditions needed for natural conception. The aim is to diagnose and correct the cause. A couple with a treated, identified cause has a different starting point than one labeled "unexplained."

### What is a NaProTechnology surgeon?
A NaProTechnology surgeon is a physician trained to perform minimally invasive gynecologic surgery within a restorative framework. The surgical evaluation is informed by Creighton Model FertilityCare System charting, which enables cycle-timed assessment before and after any procedure. Surgeons typically hold OBGYN or minimally invasive gynecologic surgery training with additional NaProTechnology fellowship preparation. Find a NaProTechnology surgeon at /providers/.
```

**/femm/** — query "what is femm"
> FEMM, which stands for Fertility Education and Medical Management, is a fertility-awareness based method that teaches women to observe and chart two biological markers: cervical mucus and the LH/estrogen hormone pattern detected through urinary testing. Unlike tracking methods used only to time intercourse, FEMM pairs cycle charting with a medical management model: when the chart reveals an abnormal pattern, FEMM-trained clinicians use that data to identify and treat the underlying cause. It is both a body-literacy tool and a clinical framework.

**/naprotechnology/** — query "how does naprotechnology work"
> NaProTechnology works by making the ovulatory cycle medically readable. A woman learns to chart her cycle using the Creighton Model FertilityCare System, recording cervical mucus observations that reflect the hormonal events driving ovulation. That chart becomes a diagnostic record: a NaProTechnology-trained clinician reads the biomarker patterns, identifies where the cycle is breaking down, orders targeted evaluations timed to the chart, and then treats the identified condition. Rather than overriding reproductive function, the entire approach cooperates with it, addressing the root cause so the body can conceive or function on its own terms.

**/faqs/what-is-the-difference-between-creighton-model-marquette-method-femm-and-symptot/** — query "creighton vs marquette nfp"
> The Creighton Model, Marquette Method, FEMM, and symptothermal methods all track fertility signals, but each uses a different biomarker. The Creighton Model observes cervical mucus only, using a standardized scoring system that also forms the foundation for NaProTechnology medical evaluation. The Marquette Method adds urinary hormone monitoring: a handheld device measures LH and estrogen, and the method can be used with or without mucus observations. FEMM tracks both cervical mucus and urinary hormones, and pairs that chart with a medical management model for diagnosing and treating underlying conditions. Symptothermal methods track two signals, basal body temperature and cervical mucus, using a double-check rule where both must confirm the fertile window has closed. Which method fits a couple best depends on what biomarker they can observe consistently, and whether medical evaluation is part of the goal.

**/faqs/how-much-does-rrm-or-naprotechnology-treatment-cost-compared-to-ivf/** — query "what is restorative reproductive medicine"
> Restorative Reproductive Medicine (RRM) is a field of medicine that works with the reproductive cycle to find and treat the underlying cause of a couple's fertility or gynecologic problem, rather than bypassing it. Where IVF sidesteps the question of why conception is not occurring and attempts to produce a pregnancy around the obstacle, RRM asks why the obstacle exists and addresses it directly. The goal is a body that functions: regular ovulatory cycles, identified disease, corrected anatomy.
