# Module 9: Hands On: Setup and First Real Conversations

Course 1: AI Foundations for the Restorative Clinician. Byline: Brian Whittaker. Status: DRAFT for Brian review.
Terms introduced: none (deliberately). This module operationalizes Modules 1-8.

## Orientation

No new vocabulary. This module gets you set up in ChatGPT and Claude and walks you through five deliberately chosen first conversations, each engineered to let you watch a concept from this course happen live: the mental model made physical. By the end you will have working accounts, a dictation habit, and firsthand experience of the exact behaviors, good and bad, the previous eight modules described. This is also the course's certificate module: the final quiz draws on everything.

## Lesson

### Setup, briskly

**Accounts.** Create free accounts at chatgpt.com and claude.ai with a work email. Free tiers are fully sufficient for this module. If you already know AI will be part of your week, one paid individual plan (either vendor, roughly $20 monthly) is justified for upper-tier access and limits (Module 7); it can wait until Course 2 makes you hungry for it.

**Apps and dictation.** Install both mobile apps and sign in. Enable the microphone and try one dictated prompt today; clinicians are almost universally faster speaking than typing, and dictation converts commute thoughts and between-patient minutes into usable drafts. Recall from Module 6: voice is the same machine through a different door, so every rule travels with it.

**Data hygiene from day one** (Module 8 made these non-negotiable; do them before the first real prompt):

1. In each tool's settings, find the data controls and set training-related options to your preference; know where they live regardless, because plans and policies change.
2. Work email, work password manager, and two-factor authentication if offered.
3. Say the rule aloud once, at your actual keyboard: patient information never goes in here. The physical setting is part of installing the habit.

**A note for this module's exercises:** everything below is PHI-free by construction. Where a prompt needs clinical texture, it uses abstracted vignettes (Module 8's pattern) or public knowledge.

### Five first conversations, each with a purpose

Run all five, in order, in either tool (or alternate; feeling both interfaces is itself useful). Each takes five to ten minutes. Do not just read them; the entire value is in watching the behavior happen under your own hands.

**Conversation 1: The transformation baseline (Modules 1, 5).**
Paste a dense paragraph from any public medical source (a society statement, a public abstract), then: "Rewrite this for a patient at an eighth-grade reading level, warm tone, under 150 words, ending with one question the patient might ask their clinician." Then iterate once: "Now as three short bullet points."
*What you are watching:* grounded transformation, the safest and highest-yield use of these tools: the model working from supplied text rather than distant memory, and taking iteration gracefully. This is the baseline experience the whole course wants you to have first.

**Conversation 2: The hallucination, on demand (Module 5).**
In a fresh chat with web search off: "List four peer-reviewed studies on [a genuinely narrow topic in your subspecialty], with full citations." Then verify all four in PubMed yourself.
*What you are watching:* whatever you find, you win. Fabricated or garbled citations make Module 5 permanently visceral. Four clean citations teach the subtler lesson: you could not have known without checking, and the checking took three minutes.

**Conversation 3: The window and the amnesia (Module 4).**
Tell the model three specific invented preferences ("I prefer bullet summaries, British spellings, and answers under 200 words"). Confirm it complies. Then open a brand-new chat and ask: "What are my formatting preferences?"
*What you are watching:* within-chat context versus between-chat amnesia, physically demonstrated. When Course 3 introduces custom instructions and memory features, you will know exactly what problem they solve.

**Conversation 4: The steering wheel (Modules 2, 4).**
Ask, in one chat: "What should a woman with irregular cycles know?" Then, in a fresh chat: "You are drafting patient education for a restorative reproductive medicine practice. The audience is a woman with irregular cycles who wants her cycle understood and treated as a vital sign, not suppressed by default. What should she know?"
*What you are watching:* Module 2's volume-weighted defaults, and how completely explicit framing redirects them. The difference between these two outputs is the single most persuasive argument for learning to prompt, and it is the bridge to Course 2.

**Conversation 5: The judgment call (Modules 5, 8).**
Give the model a de-identified vignette you write yourself (Module 8's drill: strip, generalize, adversarial reread), and ask for something squarely in the drafting lane: "Draft the questions this patient will likely ask at her next visit, with brief plain-language answers I can adapt."
*What you are watching:* yourself, running the full discipline end to end: de-identify, prompt with context, receive a useful draft, and notice which parts you would verify or rewrite before any patient saw them. This conversation, not any single answer it produces, is the course's actual graduation exercise.

### The habits to carry out of Course 1

Six sentences, the whole course:

1. It predicts text; it does not look things up (unless you turn search on).
2. Give it the document; grounded beats remembered every time.
3. One conversation per task; the window is finite and the middle goes dim.
4. Every citation and every actionable fact gets verified, scaled to stakes.
5. Patient information never enters a consumer tool; de-identify or use a covered product.
6. You review everything that matters; that is not overhead, that is the job description.

### Try it

The five conversations are the try-it. When they are done, take the certificate quiz, and then bring your three most annoying recurring writing tasks to Course 2; it opens by turning them into templates.

## Quiz: certificate assessment (10 questions, drawing on the full course)

This step is the course's certificate quiz (passing threshold per platform standard, 80). Questions 1 to 5 below; 6 to 10 are drawn from the Module 1 to 8 banks at assembly time to sample every module.

1. **MCQ.** The single mechanism underlying everything an LLM does is:
   - A. Database retrieval with natural-language search
   - B. Predicting the next token given the context ✓
   - C. Rule-based expert-system logic
   - D. Live consultation of medical references
   - *Explanation: Module 1's foundation; every capability and every failure mode in this course traces back to it.*

2. **MCQ.** A colleague's chat has run for three weeks across many topics and now gives confused, degraded answers. The best diagnosis and fix:
   - A. The model has degraded; switch vendors
   - B. Context window overflow and cross-contamination; start one fresh conversation per task, carrying forward only a summary ✓
   - C. Too many regenerations raised the temperature permanently
   - D. The knowledge cutoff was reached mid-conversation
   - *Explanation: Module 4 applied: the window is finite, middles go dim, unrelated threads contaminate. Hygiene, not hardware, is the fix.*

3. **MCQ.** Which item may be pasted into a consumer AI tool under this course's rules?
   - A. A patient portal message with the name deleted but her rare diagnosis, town, and appointment date intact
   - B. An abstracted vignette: "a woman in her late 30s with recurrent early miscarriage, trying to conceive for three years" ✓
   - C. A photograph of a patient's chart with the corner torn off
   - D. Exam-room audio, if the scribe vendor has a BAA with someone
   - *Explanation: Module 8: only genuinely de-identified content crosses the line. A and C fail the adversarial reread; D confuses one vendor's BAA with coverage of a different transmission.*

4. **MCQ.** The model, without web search, produces a beautifully formatted citation supporting exactly the claim you hoped for. The professional next step:
   - A. Use it; well-formatted citations are reliable
   - B. Retrieve it yourself in PubMed before it appears anywhere with your name on it ✓
   - C. Ask the model to confirm the citation is real
   - D. Regenerate; if it appears twice, it exists
   - *Explanation: Module 5: surface perfection is what generation produces, the model cannot audit itself, and repetition is not existence. Retrieval is the only test.*

5. **MCQ.** For which task is a reasoning model with your documents in context the right escalation over a fast model?
   - A. Reformatting a handout to bullet points
   - B. Determining where two society guidelines and your practice protocol genuinely conflict, and what each would imply for a described (de-identified) presentation ✓
   - C. Drafting four versions of a recall reminder
   - D. Translating a paragraph to Spanish
   - *Explanation: Modules 6 and 7: transformations belong on the fast tier; multi-document analysis under constraints is what deliberation and grounding are for.*

## Scenario

**Setup.** It is Monday morning after finishing this course. In your inbox: (a) a patient message asking whether an AI-recommended supplement stack is safe (she pasted the chatbot's answer, which includes two citations); (b) a vendor email pitching an ambient scribe, "HIPAA-compliant, zero hallucinations, no review needed"; (c) your own to-do: rewrite the practice's post-procedure instructions at a lower reading level.

**Task.** For each item, write one or two sentences: which course concept governs it, and what is your move?

**Model answer.** (a) Module 5 governs: verify her two citations in PubMed before responding (expect at least the possibility of fabrication), then reply using the Module 5 pattern: affirm that she brought it in, report what the citations actually are, pivot to the real evidence. The reply itself can be drafted from a de-identified summary, not her pasted thread. (b) Module 7 and 8 govern: "zero hallucinations, no review needed" is a claim the technology cannot support, and "HIPAA-compliant" is marketing until a signed BAA exists; the pitch has disqualified its own credibility, and any evaluation starts with the pipeline questions and the contract. (c) Modules 1 and 5 govern, happily: this is grounded transformation, the safest lane there is. Paste the current instructions into a consumer tool (no patient data involved), specify reading level, tone, and length, iterate twice, and review the result yourself before it ships. One inbox, three lanes, all six habits in play.
